## Phile 37 - Tor

Tor stands for The Onion Router. Tor is a privacy-focused netowrk, developed from research into onion routing  at the U.S. Naval Research Laboratory in the 1990s. Tor is maintained by the Tor Project, a non-profit organization responsible for its development, was founded in 2006.
Tor is a distributed network designed not only to protect traffic through layered encryption, but also to obscure the relationship between the user and the destination. Tor Browser also aims to make users more difficult to distinguish from one another by reducing browser fingerprinting. Therefore, Tor can help to provide online anonimity altough it cannot gurantee it.

### How does it work?

When browsing the web through tor, traffic passes through 3 nodes before reaching its destination: entry node, middle node and exit node. This path is called Tor circuit. The network is designed that no single node normally has access to both the user's identity and the final destination. The entry node knows the the user's IP address and the next relay in the circuit, but it does not know the final destination. The middle node knows the previous and the next relays but not the original user or the final destination. The exit node knows the destination it connects to, but it does not have any information about the origin of the traffic. When using Tor, the ISP sees that the user is connecting to the Tor network, but it cannot directly see the webisted is being visited through the Tor circuit.  The destination website sees a connection originating from the exit relay rather than the user's real IP address. However, this does not prevent a website from identifying a user who logs into a personal account or reveals identifying information in other ways.

it is also important to note that Tor protocol does not automatically encrypt the traffic all the way to the destination. If a website does not use HTTPS the traffic leaving the exit node may be exposed to the relay operator or other observers on the network path.

### Accessing the Tor network

Users can access the Tor network through the Tor Browser, a privacy focused, hardened browser based on Mozilla Firefox. it includes prtoection against tracking and browser fingerprinting. Tor also supports websites designed specifically for the network, known as onion services. Their addresses end in .onion, and they can only be accessed through Tor. Unlike regular websites accessed through an exit relay, onion services keep the connection within the Tor network and provide end-to-end encryption as part of their protocol.

### Advantages and Limitations of Tor

One of Tor's main advantages is its distributed architecture. Unlike traditional VPN which requires users to place significant trust ina single provider, tor distributes trust into multiple relays. No single relay should normally be able to identify both the user and the final destination. 
Keep in mind Tor is not 100% bulletproof. For example, an adversary capable of observing traffic at both ends of a connection may be able to use timing and traffic-correlation analysis to infer a relationship between the user and the destination. Malicious relays, browser vulnerabilities, user mistakes, and revealing personal information can also undermine anonymity.

Tor therefore provides meaningful privacy and anonymity protections, but its effectiveness depends on the threat model and how it is used.


### Final Takeaway

Although Tor is sometimes criticized over questions concerning its security and trustworthiness, it remains an important tool in many OPSEC strategies. It helps protect the relationship between a user's identity and online activity by distributing trust across multiple relays instead of relying on a single centralized provider.
Ultimately, Tor is a tool, not a guarantee of anonymity. Its effectiveness depends on understanding its limitations, avoiding operational mistakes, and choosing an approach that fits your threat model.
