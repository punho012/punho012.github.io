### Introduction

The rapid growth of instant messaging applications cannot be denied. Protecting these communcations channels and maintaining privacy and - under stricter threat model - anonimity have become essential. 
Today, many messaging applications offer encryption but not all provide end-to-end encryption (E2EE) by default. Without E2EE, service providers may access private message contents. E2EE addresses this issue by ensuring that only the intended participants have the keys needed to read the messages. 
Always check whether E2EE is enabled by default. If an application does not provide it, consider whether it is suitable for your threat model. 
However, E2EE is enabled, observers may still be able to identity the sender and recipient through metadata. That's why it is worth considering the threat model when choosing messaging application to use.

### Factors for choosing the most apprpriate messaging app

**Minimum requirements (for privacy):**
1. The application must be free and open source (FOSS).
2. The application must provide end-to-end encryption by default (E2EE).
3. The application must support Perfect Forward Secrecy, which helps protect previously exchanged messages if long-term encryption keys are compromised later. 

**Advanced requirements(for anonymity):**
1. The application must support Tor.
2. The application must allow users to communicate without requiring personally identifying information such as phone numbers or email address.
3. The application must allow users to operate their own servers (Decentralization).

**Additional considerations (for deniablity):**
1. Disappearing messages can reduce the amount of conversation history retained on devices, but they do not guarantee deniability. Recipients may still save, copy, or photograph messages.

### Some recomended applications

Here are several applications worth considering. None of them meets every requirement for every threat model.

- **Signal:** a widely used messanger with defaukt E2EE and strong securtiy properties but account registration still requires a phone number.
- **SimpleX Chat:** open-source, designed to operate without permanent user identifiers and offers E2EE, with support for connecting through Tor.
- **Briar:** an open-source, peer-to-peer messaging application, designed for situations where cnesorship resistance and communication without centralized servers are important. 
- **Session:**an E2EE messanger that uses onion routing and does not require a phone number or email address to create an account.

Before choosing an application familiarize yourself with its architecture, securtiy model, metadata handling and limitations. Always conduct your own research to determine which applications best fits for your threat model.

### Final takeaway

Choosing a secure messaging application is not simply about finding the strongest encryption. It is about understanding what information the application protects, what metadata it exposes, and how much trust you must place in its infrastructure.

End-to-end encryption protects the content of your conversations, but it does not automatically protect your identity, relationships, or communication patterns. Depending on your threat model, you may also need to consider metadata minimization, anonymity, identity separation, and the trust placed in service providers.
