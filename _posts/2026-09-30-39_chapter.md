### Introduction

Many of our private communications still rely on electronic channles such as email. However, email was not originally designed with strong privacy and confidentiality in mind. Therefore, email messages are easy to intercept and scan for interesting keywords. This can be done easily, routinely, automatically, and undetectably on a grand scale.
Email providers such as Google, Yahoo process email data for various purposes like advertising and training their AI. The fact that these communications are readable by employees (even if only certain ones) means that any sensitive information is not safe and can be potentially stolen.
One way to improve email security is through tools such as OpenPGP, which add end-to-end encryption and digital signatures to email communication. Email aliases can also help protect your primary address and reduce unnecessary expoasure.

### Email Metadata Overview

Even if users protect their privacy with different tool and tecniques, users are still exposed to email metadata. Email metadata is stored in the message header of the email message and includes some visible headers that you may have seen such as To, From, Cc, Date, and Subject. There are also additional headers that can reveal reveal information including mail servers, IP addresses, and routing fetails.
This information can expose communication patterns, reltionships, and technical details even when the message content itsel is encrypted.

### Why Do You Need PGP?

PGP is a cryptographic approach used to protect files and communications. PGP is an open standard commonly used for implementing PGP-compatible encryption. I provides end-to-end encryption, allowing only the intended recipient with the appropriate private keys to decrypt the message. It can also provide digital signatures, which help verify the sender's identity and ensure message integrity.
However, PGP does not automatically encrypt every part of an email. Metadata such as sender and recipient addresses, timestamps, and usually the subject line can remain exposed.

### Email Aliases

Nowadays most online services use email addresses as usernames. Reusing the same email address across multiple platforms makes it easier to correlate accounts and increases the impact of a potential data breach. Email aliases are a simple but effective way to reduce this exposure.
An email aliasing service allows you to easily generate a new email address for every website you register for. Incoming messages are then forwarded to an email address of your choice, keeping your primary address hidden.
This also makes it easier to identify where unwanted emails or spam originate. If one of your aliases starts receiving suspicious messages, you can disable it without changing your primary email address.
Email aliasing can also act as a safeguard in case your email provider ever ceases operation. In that scenario, you can easily re-route your aliases to a new email address. In turn, however, you are placing trust in the aliasing service to continue functioning.

### Privacy focused email services
There are several privacy-focused email providers worth considering. Two examples are:
- **Proton Mail:** based in Switzerland, Proton is one of major providers in the privacy space. Messages between Proton users are automatically end-to-end encrypted. It also supports OpenPGP-compatible encryption for communication with external users.
- **Tuta Mail:** based in Germany. Tuta uses its own end-to-end encryption architecture rather than OpenPGP. It encrypts additional mailbox information, including subject lines, although some information required for email delivery, such as email addresses and timestamps, remains visible.

Both services offer privacy protections but their encryption models and compatibility differ. Choosing between them should depend on your threat model, particularly whether interoperability with standard email providers and OpenPGP support are important to you.

### Final takeaway

Email remains one of the most widely used communication channels, but convenience should not be confused with privacy. Standard email does not provide end-to-end encryption by default, and even when encryption is introduced, metadata can still expose valuable information about your communications.

PGP protects the content of your messages, while email aliases help protect your identity and reduce account correlation. Privacy-focused email providers offer additional protections, but they also introduce their own trust assumptions and technical limitations.

Ultimately, securing email is not about finding a single tool that solves every problem. It is about combining encryption, metadata awareness, identity separation, and trustworthy services according to your threat model.
