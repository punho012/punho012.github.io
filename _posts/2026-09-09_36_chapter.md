## Phile 36: VPN

VPN stands for Virtual Private Network. It creates an encrypted connection between your device and the VPN provider’s server.

### How does it work actually?
Normally, IPSs can observe web traffic entering and leaving their networks. Altough, modern web traffic relies heavily on encryption technologies such as HTTPS which prevents the ISP from seeing the content of communications, they can still observe metadata, such as destination IP address and in some circumstances DNS requests. 
VPNs changes this by encrypting the traffic between the user and the VPN server.
The traffic is encrypted between the VPN client and the VPN server. From here, the provider routes the traffic to the destination. The traffic between the VPN server and the destination is not automatically encrypted, which is why HTTPS remains crucial when accessing websites and online services.

### Advantage of using VPNs

VPNs offer privacy by encrypting the traffic between the user and the VPN server. Since traffic is routed through the VPN server, the user's ISP sees a connection to the VPN provider rather than the final destiantion of the traffic.
From the perspective of the destination website, the connection appears to originate from the VPN server, which obscures the user's real IP address from that website. 
It also geographically shifting network traffic, therefore users can avoid geographic-based restrictions and censorships.

### Disadvantage of using VPN

VPN does not provide anonimity because the VPN provider becomes the new point of trust. The provider can still potentially see the user's real ip address and the connections going through its infrastructure. Depending on the service and payment method, additional information may also be associated with the user. Therefore, choosing a VPN provider requires careful consideration of its logging practices, jurisdiction, infrastructure, and overall trustworthiness.

A "no-logging policy" is ultimately a claim made by the provider. Depending on the threat model, additional measures may therefore be necessary. Tor can be used in addition to a VPN, or instead of one, when the threat model requires stronger anonymity properties.

Using a VPN does not eliminate trust — it shifts the trust to the VPN provider.

### Final takeaway

VPNs provide a way to hide your real IP address from the destination you are connecting to and to protect traffic between your device and the VPN server. However, this does not eliminate the need for trust. Instead, it shifts the trust from your ISP and other network-level observers to the VPN provider. The VPN provider becomes a new point of trust that can potentially observe information about your connections, which is why choosing a provider should always be based on your threat model rather than marketing claims alone.

This is one of the most important principles of OPSEC: security and privacy tools do not make trust disappear — they often move where that trust is placed. If the threat model requires stronger protection, additional technologies such as Tor or stronger compartmentalization may be appropriate.
